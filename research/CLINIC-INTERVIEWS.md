# 10 clinic conversations: script and scorecard

Goal: find out, in 10 short conversations, whether independent dentists and physical therapists lose real money to no-shows and would let you text their patients. You are **not** selling. You are learning what already happens.

**Pass rule (decide before you start):** 6 or more of 10 clinics (a) name a no-show problem that costs them money, and (b) say yes to a free 30-day pilot. If not, do not build more.

## Who and how
- **Who:** independent dentists and physical therapists, 1 to 5 providers, in your city. Not friends only: at most 3 of the 10.
- **Where to find them:** walk in during a quiet hour, your local Philippine Dental Association chapter, clinics near you, a dentist or PT you know who introduces two others.
- **Time:** ask for 15 minutes. Offer to buy coffee. Speak to the doctor or whoever runs the diary.
- **Rules:** do not demo the app until the end. Do not collect patient names or numbers. Write notes right after, not during, unless they are fine with it.

## Opening (30 seconds)
English:
> "Hi Doc, I'm [name]. I'm building a small appointment book for clinics and I'm not selling anything today. I'm trying to understand how clinics handle patients who forget their appointments. Could I ask you a few questions? About 15 minutes."

Taglish (adapt to your voice):
> "Hello po, Doc. Ako po si [name]. Gumagawa po ako ng simpleng appointment book para sa mga clinic. Hindi po ako nagbebenta ngayon, gusto ko lang pong matutunan kung paano ninyo hinahandle yung mga pasyenteng nakakalimutan ang appointment. Pwede po ba kayong tanungin ng mga 15 minutes?"

## Questions (in this order)
Ask about **what happened**, not what they would do. If you get a vague answer, ask "can you tell me about the last time?"

**1. Their world**
1. How do patients book with you today? (phone, Messenger, walk-in, a receptionist?)
2. Who keeps the diary, and where? (paper, phone calendar, an app)
3. About how many patients do you see in a week?

**2. The problem, in numbers and stories**
4. Last week, how many booked patients did not show up or cancelled at the last minute? (Get a number. "Maybe 3" is fine.)
5. Tell me about the last time it happened. What did you do with that time?
6. What does an empty slot cost you? (Ask the fee, or what the chair or hour earns.)
7. How much does it bother you? Is it a big deal or just part of the job?

**3. What they do now**
8. Do you remind patients before the visit? How? (call, text, Viber, Messenger, nothing)
9. Who does it, and how long does it take each day?
10. Has it helped? How many still don't show?
11. Have you tried an app or software for this? What happened? Do you still use it? How much did it cost?
12. What do you spend each month on reminders, including load, staff time or software?

**4. Fit and trust**
13. If patients got a text, do you think any would mind? What do you tell patients about how you keep their details?
14. Who decides whether to try something new here: you, a partner, the owner?

## The test at the end (only now)
Briefly describe it in one sentence: *"The idea is that when you book a patient, they automatically get a text before the visit, from the app, so no one has to call."*

15. "Does that sound useful, or not really? Why?" (Listen for a reason, not a compliment.)
16. "I'm trying a free 30-day pilot with a few clinics. Could I set it up with you next week?" (**Yes / maybe / no.** "Maybe" counts as no.)
17. "If it worked and it cost [₱499] a month for one doctor, how would you feel about that? Too much, fair, cheap?" (Then be quiet and let them answer.)
18. "Do you know one or two other clinics who have this problem? Could you introduce me?"

Thank them, and ask if you can contact them again.

## Do and don't
- **Don't** ask "would you use this?" or "do you like it?" Everyone says yes. Ask about the last time and what they did.
- **Don't** explain the product in detail, or defend it. If they push back, write it down.
- **Do** ask "why" and "what happened next".
- **Do** count how many clinics ask "when can I try it?" That is the strongest signal.

## How to read it
**Strong signals:** a specific number and a recent story; money lost; they already tried to fix it (staff time, an app); they ask to try it; they name a price without flinching; they offer an introduction.
**Weak signals:** "interesting", "good idea", "sounds nice", "maybe later", no number, no story.

| Result | What to do |
|---|---|
| 6 or more pass | Start the pilot with the clinics that said yes. Build nothing new. |
| 3 to 5 pass | Look at who said yes. If they share a type (dentists only, or clinics with a receptionist), narrow to them and run 10 more calls. |
| 0 to 2 pass | Stop. The problem is not painful enough, or reminders are already solved. Revisit the idea before spending more. |

Also look at what clinics **already pay** for reminders (question 12) and how they react to the price (question 17). If most say the price is too high, lower it before the pilot, not after.

## After each call (5 minutes)
Write down the exact words they used for the problem (reuse them on the landing page), their number, and what surprised you.
